# CV Paper Reading — source-order default (v1.2.1)

This skill helps a graduate reader understand a computer-vision or ML paper **in the order and argumentative structure chosen by its authors**. Reconstruct the actual paper's main sections and their roles before drafting. The author’s sequence—from research question and prior approaches, through technical challenges and method choices, to evidence and conclusions—is substantive content to teach, not merely a table of contents to discard.

Read [narrative workflow](narrative-workflow.md), [reader experience](reader-experience.md), [scientific integrity](scientific-integrity.md), and task-specific evidence guides. In schema **1.3**, create the original-paper `reader.roadmap` in actual main-section order. Author a concise `reader.argument_route` for the beginning of the report and map each main report `sections[].source_sections` to the original labels. In schema 1.3, `reader.throughline` and `reader.connections` are optional planning notes: do not generate a second prose plan or per-chapter dependency ledger when the source map already makes the structure clear. The default `reader.organization` is `paper-order`; a material exception must use `restructured` with a concrete `departure_reason` and still preserve chapter coverage. Older schema 1.0–1.2 artifacts remain compatible.

Keep the first screen concise (research question, method, chief results), followed by the short original-paper route. In each major section explain **why it comes next, what the authors establish, and how it leads forward**. Discuss key prior approaches and why they are inadequate before presenting a new technique. Develop mathematics, intuition and detailed figure interpretation **within the relevant original chapter**, rather than moving equations in front of the motivation. Preserve and interpret pivotal original framework and experimental figures when accessible; add interactive figures only for a real teaching gain.

Distinguish three layers: (1) faithful reconstruction of the author's argument in order, (2) technical deepening placed beside the relevant section, and (3) a concentrated independent critique **after** the author's main argument. Present only conclusion-changing qualifications beside their evidence; do not litter the article with repeated defensive caveats. Technical logs and full evidence ledgers belong in `analysis-notes.md`.

Once the report is built, do one quick combined check of actual source chapter coverage, key formulas/evidence, major explanatory transitions, and delivered artifacts. Validate with `--require-artifacts` (add `--md-only` for Markdown-only). Do not require first-quarter comprehension pauses, chapter-by-chapter blind retellings, extra reader calls, `reader-review.json`, or repeated QA rounds. The optional `prepare_reader_review.py` / `--require-reader-review` workflow is only for explicitly requested reader studies. No routine pytest, browser, training or reproduction runs.
